# Convexity

A mobile-first trading-desk game. You run the cross-asset exotic derivatives book at Halden Wray, a fictional bank in London. Price client RFQs, warehouse the risk, hedge what you can't afford to keep, and learn why a trade that looks profitable can still blow up.

Built with React, TypeScript and Vite. No backend. Every market, client and number is generated by a seeded simulation.

```bash
cd convexity
npm install
npm run dev        # http://localhost:5173 (served on your LAN too, so you can open it on your phone)
npm test           # 26 tests: pricing, exotics, accounting, quoting, determinism, scenarios
npm run build      # type-check + static build in dist/
```

## Playing

- **The floor is a chat feed.** Colleagues show up there:
  - Sales (Priya) brings RFQs.
  - The strategist (Jules) gives the morning brief.
  - Margot, your head of desk, mentors you and writes the debrief.
  - Risk (Dmitri) enforces limits.
  - The quant (Wen) explains models.
  - The structurer (Tomás) pitches notes.
  - The flow vol trader (Theo) offers inter-desk liquidity.

  Buttons in messages do real things: hedge, open a ticket, accept an axe.
- **The risk strip** across the top shows P&L, delta, gamma, vega, theta, correlation, DV01 and VaR live, with limit usage. Tap any chip.
- **The dock** opens the panels: Markets (prices, vol smiles and term structure, curve, correlation, news and calendar), Book (positions, RFQs, blotter), Hedge (suggested hedges, manual ticket, band auto-hedger), Risk (Greeks, VaR/ES, stress tests, spot × vol grid), and the Lab (autocall structurer and option explorer).
- **A trading day** lasts about 2 minutes at 1× (pause, 1×, 2×, 4×). The clock stops while a ticket or the Lab is open.
- **The end-of-day report** attributes every dollar: client edge, hedge costs, delta, gamma, vega, theta, rates, correlation, carry and unexplained. It also shows a gamma-vs-theta break-even and Margot's notes. A weekly review scores risk-adjusted P&L, client franchise, discipline and execution, and decides promotion: Junior Trader → Trader → Senior Trader → Head of Desk.
- **Modes:**
  - Career: saved automatically.
  - Challenges: five seeded scenarios with transparent scoring.
  - Sandbox: every product, regime and event controls, any seed.

## Architecture (`src/engine`, UI-free and unit-tested)

| Module | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| `math.ts` | seeded PRNG, hashing, normal CDF, Cholesky |
| `market.ts` | market state, smile, Nelson–Siegel curve, correlation, tick dynamics, regimes |
| `events.ts` | scheduled calendar (Fed, CPI, ECB, payrolls, earnings), unscheduled shocks, news and rumours |
| `pricing.ts` | BSM / Black-76 / Bachelier, smile-consistent digitals, Reiner–Rubinstein barriers, worst-of autocallable Monte Carlo, swaps, forwards |
| `risk.ts` | bump-and-revalue Greeks (Δ Γ ν vanna volga θ DV01 ρ), delta-gamma-vega VaR/ES, full-revaluation stress |
| `book.ts` | positions, execution costs, lifecycle (barriers, expiries, autocall observations, swap coupons), P&L buckets and Greek explain |
| `clients.ts` | clients, RFQ generation, quote ↔ edge conversions, competing dealers, acceptance |
| `hedging.ts` | listed hedge universe, delta / delta-gamma / vega / DV01 solvers |
| `career.ts` | levels, permissions, limits, weekly review |
| `game.ts` | orchestrator: tick loop, NPC triggers, limits and forced reductions, day close |
| `scenarios.ts` | challenges |
| `learn.ts` | 21 concepts, each at three depths (intuition / trader / quant) plus a live reading of your book |

The UI (`src/ui`) is a thin layer over the engine. The Lab's 16k-path Monte Carlo runs in a Web Worker (`src/workers/lab.worker.ts`).

**Determinism.** Every random draw comes from a stream keyed on (seed, day, tick, purpose). The market path is identical whatever the player trades, and that is tested. Event outcomes are keyed on the event id, never on positions.

## Models and their assumptions

- **Dynamics.** Equities and FX follow a log-OU stochastic volatility process. Spot–vol correlation is −0.75 for ORX, plus negatively skewed systemic jumps and a latent daily drift that informed clients partly see. Regimes (calm, trending, choppy, stressed, crisis) follow a Markov chain pushed by a latent stress variable, which shows up in credit spreads and rumours.
- **Implied vol.** ATM vol is the expected average variance, plus a regime-dependent variance risk premium, plus scheduled event variance. The smile is quadratic in standardised moneyness and sticky-moneyness: it moves with spot, so delta includes a skew term.
- **Barriers.** Continuous monitoring, flat vol at the strike, checked every 5 minutes in the lifecycle. A local-vol model would price down-and-ins higher under skew.
- **Autocallables.** Monte Carlo on observation dates only, correlated GBM, flat vol per name (smile at the spot/KI midpoint), quanto drift for AUR, discounting at the curve plus an 85bp issuer funding spread. The knock-in is European. Barriers are smoothed over a 2% band for stable Greeks; settlement uses the hard barriers. No stochastic or local vol and no correlation skew, so the price would move under a richer model.
- **Rates.** Single-curve. The floating leg accrues the short rate continuously.
- **Futures.** Prepaid forwards. No margining.
- **VaR.** Taylor approximation (delta, gamma, vega, vanna, volga), so it misses barrier jumps. Stress tests use full revaluation.
- **Competitors.** Rival dealers quote around a street margin with model disagreement. Sales' hit probability comes from the distribution, not the actual draws.
