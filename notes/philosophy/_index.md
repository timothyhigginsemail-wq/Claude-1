# Philosophy

The argument now has three natural stages:

## 1–6: Establishing understanding

*What exists, how we experience it, and how we develop reliable beliefs.*

1. **[[Existence and uncertainty]]**: Something exists—at minimum, experience is occurring—but we cannot conclusively establish its ultimate nature.
2. **[[Distinction]]**: Experience contains distinguishable content, regardless of its underlying source.
3. **[[Structure and regularity]]**: Experience exhibits patterns, relationships, and apparent laws, although their ultimate origin remains uncertain.
4. **[[Conditions of comprehension]]**: Understanding experience requires logical and cognitive structures, without necessarily establishing that these structures characterize ultimate reality.
5. **[[Prediction and reliability]]**: Observed regularities allow us to construct models whose reliability can be evaluated through their predictive success, although past success cannot guarantee future results.
6. **[[Justified belief and action]]**: The demonstrated reliability of our methods provides a basis for rationally justified beliefs that can guide action, without requiring absolute certainty.

## 7–8: Understanding ourselves

*What we appear to be and why we possess particular desires and motivations.*

7. **[[Human existence]]**: The available evidence strongly indicates that we are biological human beings shaped by evolution.
8. **[[Evolution and motivation]]**: Evolution shaped our drives toward survival, reproduction, pleasure, attachment, belonging, status, autonomy, and exploration, but their evolutionary origins do not establish which goals we should pursue.

## 9: Deciding how to live

*Which motivations and goals are worth pursuing, what constitutes flourishing, and how we should act.*

9. **[[Practical philosophy]]**: By understanding our nature, evaluating what we value, and applying reliable knowledge, we can develop rational principles for living well.
