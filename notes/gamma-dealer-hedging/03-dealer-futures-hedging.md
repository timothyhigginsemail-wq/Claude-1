# How This Drives Dealer Futures Hedging

Market makers (dealers) want zero directional risk (Net Delta = 0), so they offset their options book with S&P 500 futures and rebalance as the market moves:

* Dealers Are Long Gamma (Customers Sold Options to Dealers):
   * Mechanism: Any rally makes the dealer's net book too long (either their Long Calls grew, or their Long Puts faded and left them with excess long futures hedges). Any dip makes their net book too short.
   * Forced Hedge: Sell rallies, buy dips.
   * Market Impact: Acts as a shock absorber—suppressing volatility and pinning the index near heavy strikes.
* Dealers Are Short Gamma (Customers Bought Options from Dealers):
   * Mechanism: Any rally makes the dealer's net book too short (their Short Calls blew up, or their Short Puts faded and left them with excess short futures hedges). Any dip makes their net book too long.
   * Forced Hedge: Buy rallies, sell dips.
   * Market Impact: Acts as an accelerant—chasing momentum and expanding intraday trends.
